Rovereto to North Cape

Scroll the story to trace the ride. Choose a map stop to jump to its chapter.

This summer I participated in NorthCape4000 – an organized ultradistance cycling event. There are more than 300 participants, and to qualify as a finisher, you need to complete the provided 4000 km route on your bike in 22 days. It means, that on average, you have to ride 180 km daily.

The event started on the 23rd of July in Rovereto in Italy and finished at the most northern point of continental Europe – North Cape in Norway. Route crossed many countries: Austria, Switzerland, Germany, Chechia, Sweden and Finland. During the event, I had many challenges and difficulties, but I enjoyed it a lot: I met many people, I rested from my work and daily routine, and in general – I did not have to think about anything except where to eat and where to sleep.

Out of the Alps

Days 1–3 · Rovereto, Italy

The first day we rode in a very beautiful valley, surrounded by vineyards and green mountains. The first 100km were intensive, but very easy. And in second half of the day difficulties started: Alps. We had to climb a huge mountain, and at the same time it started to rain. By the plan, I had to climb and go down until sunset, to avoid sleeping on top, since it's very cold, wet and windy there. But climbing under the rain took much more time than expected. When I arrived to the top, I was absolutely wet and it was getting dark and cold. I decided to stop as soon as I find a suitable place. During the night it had to rain, so I needed to find a spot with some kind of roof, what is very hard to do in darkness. In the end, I stayed near the barn in field: I could not enter inside, but it had a small piece of roof outside. Not perfect, but very awaited sleep.

Second day started from small rain. As usual, everything is wet in mountains, and also as usual, all the supermarkets are closed on Sunday in Austria, so I had to eat snacks I had from the previous day. It was very cold and I had to put on all the dry clothes I had. But in afternoon sun appeared and I could put on wet clothes to dry it.

In a few hours it became very hot, more than 35 degrees, and I reached the bottom of the second huge mountain I had to climb. By this time all the clothes I had was completely dry. I can say, that it was the hardest day of the trip: climbing that mountain took me around 3 hours, since I could not ride under the sun and had to walk and rest every time I reached a shadow. I was so excited when it was over!

And the next 20 kms were one of the most beautiful parts of the trip: Austrian and Bavarian villages, surrounded by mountains. According to original plan I had to arrive to Munich that day, but since I completed smaller distance in previous day, I could not do it. I had a choice to either stay in the field again, or find a hotel, and I was very lucky to find one, which is not very expensive and just 20 km ahead. Finally I could wash all the clothes and take a warm shower – I appreciated it very much, since I did not know when will be next time I have it again.

On third day I did not have huge mountains anymore, and the morning was amazing – first 20 kms I rode in pine forest, and by afternoon I arrived to Munich – our first checkpoint. By this time sun went up and it became very hot – again around 35 degrees. It probably was the hottest week of the year there. That's why after Munich I did many stops, mostly near rivers and parks. At some point I reached area with fields and no shadow from trees, and it was very hard to ride.

I stopped near some river and spent a couple of hours under the bridge and decided that it's better to ride a couple more hours during the night instead. It was very good decision – I felt how much easier it is to ride with comfortable temperature. Route had a lot of hills ahead and I really enjoyed all of them. I did not plan to stop right after sunset, and since I had a lot of energy, I rode until 2 that night and stayed for a couple of hours for sleep in gazebo near the road. I felt I can ride even more, but it had to rain soon, so I made decision to continue in morning. I did not sleep well, but at least I was dry and had enough food for the breakfast.

Heat, hills and Berlin

Days 4–7 · Berlin, Germany

On next day I woke up at 6, quickly had breakfast, packed my things and realized that I made a hole in my sleeping bag – there was a small nail on the bench where I slept. I hot fixed it with electric tape I had for cases like this and started the ride. In short, this day also was very hard. I think one of the hardest in this trip. First half of the day route passed very long straight bike lanes, sometimes without asphalt – it was not physically hard, but mentally. Second half was worse: starting with 2 huge hills, and then dozens of smaller ones. And wind all the time. At least it was not raining.

By the end of the day I crossed the border with Czechia and hills were finally over. There was a perfect bike road in the forest, which led to Cheb – the small city we crossed in Chechia. I arrived there right after sunset, and all the cheap cafes were closed already. I was very hungry, and got so happy when found McDonald's just 2 kilometers away. When I came there I met 2 more participants, but they just finished eating and was going to continue their ride. I stayed there for an hour at least – to rest and to charge my devices.

Another thing that made me happy is that self-service kiosk had Ukrainian language option. Translation was not great, but still it's nice to see something that reminds you home. On the street I also heard many people talking Ukrainian. I think many of them had to move there after russia invaded our country. I decided to stay in the city for a night. It was around 22:30 already, and I found only 1 hotel which had a room available and luckily it was just 200 meters away.

I rested very well, charged everything and had a very good breakfast – it was included in the price. I knew it was going to rain that day, but I did not expect that it will start that early – just 15 minutes after I left the city. I put on a raincoat but rain was so hard that it did not help much. I literally had a puddle on the front of my rain coat. But the worst situation was with my shoes. There was so much water inside – like I put them into river and put them on.

In around an hour I found a bus stop and decided to rest a little. The same time rain calmed down. I put off my raincoat since I was completely wet anyway, and decided to change at least a t-shirt to warm up a little. When rain completely stopped I continued a ride and realized, that if I waited that hour in the hotel, or somewhere right after rain started, I would not lost that much time and would not that wet. But it is what it is and I continued riding with water in my shoes.

Ahead there was a mountain, I overtook it and arrived to Kraslice – another Czech town. By that time clouds went away and sun appeared. I used this opportunity to dry my clothes a little. I set like a homeless on a bench right in front of city hall. It was so nice to put off those wet shoes. I even poured some water out of them. The worst thing, that I did not have other option than continue riding with them. But I expected that it will happen sooner or later, so it was fine. In a few minutes another participant got there and did exactly the same.

Chechia, and especially that town reminded me Ukraine a lot. First of all, our languages are a bit similar, so I could read most of things written (for example, "city hall" label, different signs and ads). Second is architecture. This town had a lot soviet style buildings from 70-80s years of last century. I can't say they look nice, but it was so similar to what we have in Ukraine. And I can say the same about german cities I passed, since the route was made through eastern part, which was occupied by soviet union for a while.

Despite all these adventures in the morning, it was a really good and productive day. I rested an hour under the sun and continued the ride. Ahead there was one more hill and after passing it I entered Germany again, and after it I mostly had downhills. That day I rode until late night again, around 230 kilometers, and don't remember where exactly I stayed for a night, but somewhere behind the trees along the road.

On 6th day my minimal plan was to pass a checkpoint in Berlin and ride at least 50 km more, since I had a ferry to Sweden the next evening. I don’t remember much about first half of the day, but last 30 km to Berlin were extremely hard to do. There was a very long straight flat road (I guess it was build after second world war when Berlin was surrounded by soviet union). It was that hard physically – maybe a little, since my ass was hurting a lot by that moment. But mentally it was almost impossible.

I arrived at checkpoint at Brandeburg gate around 17:30, and after that I wanted to rest for some time. I met other participants there, and many of them planned to stay there for the night – must be they did not plan to take ferry next day. Since it was the last big city before I go to Scandinavia, I decided to buy warm clothes there. First I went to Decathlon in city center. What surprised me in Berlin is how much people use bikes there. There are a lot of bike racks, but all of them were taken. It was challenging to find where to leave my bike when I go to the shop.

Unfortunately, I could not buy things I needed there, since they don’t sell them in summer. I asked my collegue, who lives in Berlin what big sport shop he can recommend – he sent me a few options and I went there. I was a really big shop, I could buy warming things for my legs and neck – but I had to ask staff to find it for me, since they hide it for summer as well. I also needed a compact jacket, but I found that there are many decathlon stores near Stockholm, and I will be able to buy it there once I pass the city.

After that I wanted to eat dinner and I went to kebab cafe. Not surprisingly, they did not accept cards and I was happy I took some cash with me. Once I finished eating, I went to REWE supermarket nearby and bought all the necessary things for the rest of the day and next morning. And that was it – in total I spent a bit more that an hour in Berlin and I had to continue moving. The way out of the city was not comfortable, since I had to ride on very bad old bike paths. But surprisingly, I saw a lot of people using them – maybe it’s ok if you ride a little, but my ass could not handle it and I wished to get out of city as soon as possible.

On my way from Berlin I was passing many small cities, and architecture there reminded me other post-comunist cities, but much better maintained – all of these buildings looked very nice. First hour was not very comfortable since I had to share the narrow road with cars, but later there were a lot of bike roads and smaller roads without many cars. After sunset I passed a couple of interesting towns, one of them is Lidow (Mark). I don’t know why, but it was very interesting going through them. In total, I did almost 90 km after Berlin. It took me some time, but around midnight I found some good places behind trees near the road, so I did not have to deviate from the route and could save some time.

Even though I did more than initially planned that day, I still had to ride quite a lot my last day in Germany – around 150 km to Rostock. I had ferry at around 21 – it’s not that early and I had plenty of time to get there in time, but still I worried a bit, since I was very tired and did not sleep well last 2 nights. Also I was running out of electricity – my powerbank was discharged, by bike computer would last only half of the day, and one of my 2 phones also turned off. Without proper navigation I could not follow the route and it was the biggest risk for me.

After a few hours I met another participant, 50 years old man from England, who lives in Spain. We talked and rode a little and it as very nice. He was way faster than me, and I had to push harder. Thanks to that, I managed to ride some kilometers faster than I would do it alone. Also he told me about the chamois cream – I can use it for my ass, and I really needed it.

I do not remember much from the second half of the day, but similar to Berlin – last 20 km were on straight, flat narrow road, and this time shared with cars. It was very uncomfortable to ride, but at least I did not have to check map – I knew that I had to follow this road until I enter Rostock. Once I entered Rostock, I could relax a little, since the hardest part was behind – I only had to get to the port.

In the port, I met other participants waiting for the ferry, including that man I met before. There also was another guy who could not book a ticket, but in the end, somehow he was able to get on to the ferry. While waiting I charged all my devices and started a conversation with one guy. He was not a participant, just another passenger. He was from Malmo. I checked my route and found that I was going to pass this city. He invited me to his apartment so I could take a shower, wash my clothes, and rest a bit. Of course I agreed – it was something I needed a lot. When it was time to set out for the ferry, all the participants packed their things and headed to ticket control. Accidentally, that older guy took my bike computer since I was charging it using his power adapter. Luckily, I noticed it in time and was able to find him quickly.

For me, all this experience with the ferry was very interesting, since I’ve never done it before. The tricky part was to find our lane. The woman on the ticket control told us its number (0, as I remember), but we could not find it on any signs, so we just headed to the front of the queue. It was 3 of us in the group – me, that British and one Australian guy. Since they both were older and native English speakers, they started the conversation, and I only listened to them. While waiting I was worried that we were standing in the wrong place, and asked a port worker where should we go. He told me to stay where we are, and that he would tell us when we should start moving. I could relax.

In a few minutes ferry arrived and we saw many cars and trucks exiting – I did not expect this ferry to have so high capacity. When everyone left, the port worker gave us a signal that we could go to the entrance. When we entered the ferry, the worker showed us a special place where we could put our bikes. It had some slings to tie up the bikes, but they were in really bad condition, so we used them just as ropes.

Once we finished with the bikes, we went upstairs to the reception for check-in. There we had to receive the keys to our rooms. I booked a sleeping capsule, and these guys were able to book a room and share the price. I immediately asked them if I could take a shower and they agreed, but they did not tell me their room number after some time I found one of them in the common area, and he told me the room number and that I could go there since another guy was inside. But in the end, he did not open the door. So my only hope was that I would be able to take a shower in Malmo.

I also pre-booked the dinner, so I headed to the canteen. It was a bit expensive, but I knew that I would appreciate it once I got there. The food was nice. I quickly ate and went to my capsule, since I was exhausted and wanted to sleep. It was around 11 o'clock, and the arrival was scheduled for around 6 in the morning. I did not have much time to rest, so I wanted to use every minute of it. I fell asleep immediately.

The Swedish stretch

Days 8–12 · Stockholm, Sweden

Sometime before arrival, I heard the alarm clock outside, but I was so tired that I ignored it. But after some time worker came to me and asked me to wake up immediately since we had already arrived. I woke up and packed my things in 2 minutes. When I exited the capsule, I saw that all the people had already left my floor and I was the last one. I really used every minute to sleep. I could only quickly go to the bathroom, leave my key in the reception, and then I went downstairs to my bike. By that time all other cyclists left and I was the last one. I still was very tired and dreamed only about the shower.

Once I left port and moved on the route, I noticed that the scenery did not change compared to Germany – there were not any trees, only fields, and that surprised me. After 2 hours I finally arrived at the address in Malmo, and that guy let me in – I was so happy about it. He was so nice to me. I took a shower and even washed and dried my clothes. It was a really interesting experience – first of all, because I did not expect it to happen. But also there were some unusual details in the building where he lived. The apartment was very small, especially the bathroom and shower, and the common area as well – all reminded me of old soviet khruschivkas. Also, there was one common laundry in the basement, so all the people should book in using a special stand. Luckily, it was available when I was there.

I rested for a few hours and set out for my route. I had a long day ahead since I had ridden only around 30 km. On the one hand, I spent so much time there, but on the other, I rested a lot, so I had more power to cycle. On the exit from the city, I went to McDonald's, quickly ate to get more energy, and continued moving. I can’t say that the ride was easy, but not that hard. The scenery slowly changed, there were more and more trees and villages, it was interesting, but I don’t remember much from that day.

When the sun started to set, I checked the map to find a good spot for me to stay for the night but did not find anything really good nearby, so I decided to continue moving until I saw something. By that moment I already had ridden my minimal distance for the day, so I could relax a bit, but I felt that I had enough energy to keep moving, and I did it. I rode for 10 more kilometers, checked the map, and found that in 10 km right on the route there was a small town with a hotel, where a few free rooms were left. I decided it was a good option, and went directly there.

When I arrived I realized that it was an IKEA hotel, and it was the town where the company was founded. Once I entered the hotel, at the reception they immediately asked if I was part of that big group of cyclists – I was not the first one who decided to stay there. They also told me that there is a museum, and it’s free for the hotel guests. That moment I was not sure if I should go there since I had limited time. I also asked where I could leave my bike, and they showed me the room, which was locked, and to open it you should ask the receptionist. I liked it, since it’s much more secure. In the room, I saw many bikes – mostly from other participants.

The room was quite basic, but in good condition – in IKEA style. I put all my electronic devices on charge and went to bed immediately. In the morning, I quickly packed my things, ate a nice breakfast which was included in the price, and decided that I should go to the museum – I don’t know when I will be here next time, and in the long run, these 2 hours won’t make much difference. I liked it – it was interesting to read about the history of the brand and see how furniture and homes in general looked many years ago. I also found the same chairs we had at home and the keychain Yuliia has.

I spent around an hour there, then took my bike and continued the journey. This day I saw even more Scandinavian beauty – lakes, forests, and small villages. But I regretted a lot that I had not bought a chamois cream yet. It caused some difficulties.

Before my trip started, I learned that in both Sweden and Finland, there are a lot of shelters in natural places. In Sweden, they are called “Vindskydd”. I saved a map of them, but unfortunately, there were not many of them along my route. But that day I was lucky – there was one shelter right on the route and right on the perfect distance – I had to cycle a standard 180 km. Since it could be my only chance to use this kind of shelter on this trip, I decided to prepare well for the evening. I found a nice store and bought food for dinner to cook – usually, these shelters have a dedicated place for a fire and even some woods. Luckily, this one was not an exception and I had a really lovely evening. The only downside of it is that there was a hive of wild bees under the room, half a meter from the place where I planned to sleep. Initially, I was a bit scared of it, but eventually, I just placed my sleeping mat as far as possible and fell asleep.

It was a very good night. I woke up, quickly packed everything, had breakfast, and headed back to the main road – the shelter was around 2 kilometers from the route. The morning also was very nice, I cycled through villages and small towns. At one of the stops I saw a paid shower – must be for truck drivers, but I decided not to use it since it would take too much time. I just filled by bottle with the water and continued moving.

This day was even harder – I did not have that much energy, and my ass was hurting a lot. Besides that, one new problem appeared – my rear wheel started to make a strange sound, which must be the bearings. The main fear was that I wouldn’t make it to the next big city to fix it, and I could not relax because of it. Also, on one of the stops, I forgot my speaker, and realized it only after 10 kilometers – it did not make much sense to return.

To the plan, I had to ride around 180 km that day, and if I did that, I would arrive in Norrkopping city. Since it was the middle of the summer, most of the hotels were already booked. I found the cheapest one available – it was in king style, right in the city center, breakfast included, and had a bathtub – I enjoyed it a lot. I decided to take care of the wheel the next day, and just rest till the morning.

The next morning I did not wake up very early. I had breakfast, packed my bike, and checked out from the hotel. I found a few bike shops in the town and headed to the nearest. It was a small central shop, they had a service, but unfortunately, they could not fix my problem, and advised me to go to another one in the town. It was on the outskirts of the and luckily, almost on my route. There they had much more advanced service, with a specialization on road bikes. They checked my wheel and told me that it was most likely a problem with bearings, but unfortunately, they didn’t have them in stock and couldn’t repair it. I asked how bad it was – they told me that I would make it to the end, but better to fix it as soon as possible. So, I continued my journey, still worried about my wheel.

That day I had 2 options: wake up very early, ride very fast without unnecessary stops, go to the checkpoint in Stockholm and make it to a ferry at around 20:00, or ride at a relaxed pace, get to Stockholm by evening, stay there for a night, and take ferry the next day. I chose the second option since I also had to go to Decathlon to buy a jacket and new shoes. There were many Decathlon stores in Stockholm, and I found one on my route, at the entrance to the city. I checked hotels and also found a not very expensive one 5 minutes away from the store. I followed that plan – rode at a relaxed pace, made many stops, and by evening I arrived at the hotel. I checked in, took a shower, washed my clothes, and went to bed.

The next morning, as usual, I had breakfast and then walked to Decathlon. I bought a jacket I wanted, went to another sports shop and bought new shoes. Not perfect for a ride, but the best I could get for the price. I returned to the hotel, packed everything, checked out, and set out to the checkpoint. In Stockholm, it was located in a nice bike shop. The road to there was nice – mostly on bike paths. I also saw a moveable bridge on my way.

Once arrived, I got a stamp on my Nort Cape passport and asked if they could check my wheel. They confirmed that it was the problem with the bearings, but unfortunately, they also could not replace them. But, they could replace the whole wheel. It was quite expensive – almost 300 euros, but at least I could be sure that I would make it to the end.

While they were working on my bike, I went to a very nice taco restaurant at the next building – it was so pleasant to eat something cooked, and not just sausages from a supermarket. After lunch, I went for a walk in the city center, enjoyed the views, and rested before starting the next chapter. A few hours before the ferry I returned to pick up my bike, and this time I did not forget about the cream – I purchased a big jar. The next days I was very happy that I finally got it.

After that, I quickly went to the supermarket to buy something for dinner and breakfast on the ferry and headed to the port. There I met many other participants, including Paul – the French guy I met on one of the first evenings in Germany before I accelerated. And while I relaxed in Stockholm, he had enough time to catch up. Departure was around 19:30, so we had some time before going to sleep, but unfortunately, we did not have a way to contact each other, and just stayed in our rooms. The room interior reminded me of a compartment car of the Ukrainian train – but it was private, and had a shower. After the check-in, I walked a little outside, saw the views, and returned to my room to eat dinner and rest. After the day in Stockholm I was not very tired, so could not fall asleep immediately, but still, I had a good night.

Into the Finnish forests

Days 13–16 · Parkano, Finland

We arrived in Finland now. New country and new challenges. I forgot that we were arriving in a different time zone – 1 hour ahead, and did not set my alarm properly. Nevertheless, I woke up in time and packed all my things quickly. Around 7 in the morning, we with a group of other participants left the port. Initially, we were riding at the same speed, but when we left the city, some guys continued faster, and some – including Paul – stayed behind. I was in the middle, but I needed to find the toilet as soon as possible. There was nothing organized along the road, so I did my things behind the first abandoned building I found – and I immediately started to feel much better and ready for the day. After that, I quickly ate breakfast at the bus stop and continued my journey. Most other participants who arrived at the same ferry were already ahead of me, but it did not worry me.

The first 30 kilometers were not the most pleasant. We rode on the bike path along the highway, which is safe, but a bit boring, and the weather was quite windy. But then we turned to the secondary road and continued going through the picturesque villages and small towns. We were passing beautiful forests and lakes – my mood and motivation increased a lot. At the same time, there were a lot of small hills, which made it harder physically, but much more interesting compared to a flat highway.

During the day I checked where I could stay for the next night, and found a good goal – Parkano. Hotels are quite expensive in Finland – more than 100 euros a night, so I planned to find a good place outside. Unfortunately, there were not any shelters along the route, so I had to look for something as I went.

I arrived in Parkano quite early – around 21, and had plenty of time to go to a supermarket and find a place to sleep before darkness. I bought a lot of food, ate part of it immediately since it would not fit into my bags, and headed to potential spots for sleep. The first place I found was not perfect – it was a dense park, but close to the city center. I could stay there, but since it still was light outside, I decided to continue my ride a bit more until I found something better.

In 10 minutes I found a perfect spot – a gazebo with a view of the lake, and just 200 meters from the road. There was even a public toilet nearby. Also, there were some people close to the spot, but they did not stay for long and left once it started to get darker. When I checked my GPS tracker, I realized that I made a new personal record – 241km in one day. It was one of my most productive days, considering distance and uphills, and it finished very nicely. I stayed under the roof with a beautiful lake view – luckily, there were not any mosquitos. I rested for a few hours before it got completely dark, secured my belongings, and went to sleep.

New day, the same routine. Wake up early, eat breakfast, pack everything, and continue going. I do not remember much from that day, but for sure scenery started to change gradually. Less and less people and more forests. It was one of the most beautiful parts of the trip. At some point, I even decided to stop for a few minutes, enter the forest, and just spend some time there. Everything was so green. Probably, the greenest ground I’ve ever seen – everything was covered by soft moss and other grass. I enjoyed every moment of being there. But I did not know what awaited me later.

As usual, during the day I checked the map for places where I could stay for the next night, and luckily – I thought – there was a shelter at the perfect distance on my route. It had to rain in the night and the next few days, so I had to find something with a roof. A few kilometers before that shelter I found an open barn – it was not perfect, but it had some space on the floor enough for me to sleep. But I decided to keep going and use the shelter I found before.

By the evening it started drizzling, but it was not a problem – I was about to arrive at my perfect place. As usual, the shelter was located inside the forest a few hundred meters from the road. The problem was that the path to it was fully covered by grass and was very narrow, surrounded by bushes. I like those forests, but not when I have to go through them during the rain. By the moment I arrived at the place I was completely wet, especially my shoes. Not pleasant, but not critical – at least I was already under the roof and could relax.

But a few minutes later I realized that I did a serious mistake. The forest was very wet, and the shelter was located near the small river. And because of all that there were a lot of mosquitos. I could not stay in one place even for a few seconds without one of them starting to drink my blood. Ok, I thought I could hide in my sleeping bag, but a minute after I did it I realized that it was too warm outside and I started sweating heavily. There was a choice between being attacked by mosquitos and sweating in a winter sleeping bag – neither of these was pleasant. I was struggling for a few hours, but luckily in the end I was able to sleep – maybe because the temperature went a bit down, or the mosquitos also went to sleep.

The next morning I woke up early, quickly had breakfast, and decided to leave the place as quickly as possible before mosquitos also wanted to eat something. As expected, my shoes and clothes still were completely wet, but they would get wet anyway while I was getting out of the forest – I had to get out through the same path and it was still raining a bit. I was so angry about the whole situation, but I did not have any other choice than to keep going. I can surely say that it was the worst night of the whole trip, mostly because of the wrong decision to sleep in that shelter.

Once I got to the road, I changed some of my clothes to dry, and put on my waterproof jacket, but could not do anything about my wet shoes, so I just started moving. In a few hours, a few other participants caught up with me – Tetyana from Ukraine and Matteo from Italy. Tetyana did not speak English, so it was a bit hard for them to communicate, but somehow they understood each other. Starting at that moment we were going together in a group, and in a few kilometers, we stopped at the cafe to rest, eat, and drink something hot. We spent almost an hour there and meanwhile, it stopped raining. I decided to change my wet shoes to slippers, put my shoes on the strings to the handlebar, and go like this till the evening – it was very funny. This part of the day was much more enjoyable.

In Finland, we had to plan our days more carefully since there were fewer and fewer shops and places to sleep. The next big city where we could rent an apartment to rest and charge our devices was Oulu – 320 km ahead. We needed 2 days to get there so we just split the distance into halves, and that day we had to do only around 160 km.

For me it was a bit hard to keep up with their pace, so they moved ahead, and we agreed to meet later in the evening. But at some point, I missed that they stopped in a town for a coffee and continued moving. It was getting darker already and after half an hour I noticed an interesting barn where we could stay for the night. I made sure there was enough place for all of us to stay there and sent them a message to come there and to take some napkins or toilet paper so I could put it into my wet shoes. They arrived in less than 30 minutes and also were impressed by the place. Shortly after that, it started raining again and very luckily we had a decent roof now. For dinner, Matteo warmed up the prepared pasta from a supermarket – he had a small stove with him. It was very tasty and nice to finally eat something warm.

We had a really good sleep there and woke up around 6 when it was light already. It wasn’t raining anymore, but was quite windy, so we quickly ate breakfast, packed everything, and set out. Luckily we had to go for only 160 km again. That day we mostly stayed together, even though it was quite hard for me to keep up. Matteo found and booked a nice and not-so-expensive apartment for the next night, so we had a good plan.

In a few hours, a small accident happened: when the sun rose and the temperature went up, I tried to take off my jacket without stopping, but one of the sleeves got to the bike gears. I did not fall and did not damage myself, but I could not save my jacket – the sleeve was gone. And it was sad since I bought this jacket just a few days ago. I quickly hot-fixed it with my isolation tape, so it does not lose all the down, packed it, and continued moving.

When we arrived in Oulu, I quickly went to the sports shop, since it was about to close. But the jackets there were 3 times more expensive than the one from Decathlon, so I just went to another cheap store, bought a good scotch tape, and fixed my sleeve properly. Meanwhile, Matteo and Tetyana went to the supermarket to buy food for the evening, and later we met near the building where we planned to stay. Shortly we checked it, put our bikes in the bike parking in the building basement, and went to the apartment.

I was impressed by the level of bike infrastructure in the city. There is a huge network of high-quality bike paths with proper intersections and signs and lots of bike parking.

Since it was still light, I went outside for a short walk to explore the area. When it got darker I bought some beer and returned home. There Matteo was cooking proper Italian pasta – even two. In a bit, we ate dinner and went to sleep. It was a good easy day, and we had to rest since the next day we had to do around 220 km to get to Rovaniemi – the next city on the route and checkpoint.

Above the Arctic Circle

Days 17–20 · Rovaniemi, Finland

Now there were even fewer supermarkets on the route – 1 or 2 a day, so we had to plan our food supply carefully. We had breakfast in Oulu, booked the room in Rovaniemi, and set out. We agreed that we would ride separately each at his own pace, and would meet in the evening – for me it was perfect. The only supermarket before our final point for the day was in Yli-Ii – 50 km after the start. When I arrived there, Matteo and Tetyana were already about to leave. There we also met 2 British guys – other participants. They also moved a bit faster than me but made a lot of long stops for food and coffee. Next few days we intersected quite often.

In the afternoon I entered the Lapland region and shortly started to see deer along the road. But I never could come close to them – they were always running away. I do not remember much from that day, just that I arrived at Rovaniemi before darkness and around 21 checked into our room. Shortly after that, we with Matteo went to the closest pub we found – and ordered burgers and long-awaited beers. After that hard day, it was a great feeling, and the pub itself reminded me of the one in Tromso, where we went with my friend Max on the first evening of our other trip to the north. It was hard and therefore a nice day with good weather and final.

In the morning Tetyana told us that she wanted to move faster and cover longer distances every day. We could not keep up with her, agreed to separate and she left. Soon after that, I also started my day, and Matteo needed more time to rest and pack everything. Once I left the house I immediately felt that we were far in the north – the temperature was about 10 degrees. I went to the closest cafe, ate something small there, and set out. I still had to go to the checkpoint – it was in Santa Village just outside of the city, and in less than an hour I arrived there.

I entered the building, got my pre-last stamp, and the lady in the reception told me that I could visit Santa Claus – and of course, I agreed. I went upstairs to the room where he was accepting guests – there were not that many people, probably because it was summer. We talked with him for a few minutes – he even replied to me something in Ukrainian. Then we made a phono, which I could buy later, and went outside. And there I saw at least 5 more bikes from other participants – Matteo, Tetyana, British guys and some others. I waited for them to put the stamp and talk to Santa, and then we took a photo together on the Arctic Circle line.

We stayed and talked for some time and then continued the journey as usual, each at his own pace – I was probably, the slowest. This time I did not have anything booked for the night. Matteo was invited by another Italian participant Davide to the cabin that he booked earlier, and I decided to go as long as I could. Route was going on the only available road, and the first kilometers were not very comfortable because of the number of cars, but later it became very calm. And it was a really good day, probably one of the best of the whole trip. Nice weather, beautiful sceneries, and nothing was hurting. I saw beautiful forests and lots of reindeer. I appreciated a lot the arctic nature.

Probably, because of all that I was not tired in the evening. Matteo and Davide arrived at their cabin around 21, and I continued moving for 2 more hours. I stopped only because it had to rain at night and I found a perfect spot to sleep under the roof – a small public sauna along the road near a gas station. I was very comfortable there. I did not have to hurry. I slowly put my mat and sleeping bag on the bench ate dinner and went outside to stretch my legs. I was surprised at how light it was at around 23 o'clock. I knew that it was normal above the Arctic Circle, but it was the first time in my life that I experienced that and I was very excited. It was the perfect evening after the perfect day.

The next day the weather turned a little worse with small rain – but was not critical at all. I started my ride not very early, and around 11 arrived at the commercial spot with some restaurants, where I stopped to eat proper food. I spent around 30 minutes there and when I was leaving, Matteo together with Davide caught up with me – they also planned to eat there. We talked for a few minutes and I continued my journey. By that moment I still did not know where I would stay for the night, but I was not worried about it. I only wanted to make sure I would ride as far as possible on the 70 km long west-oriented section at the end of that day since now the weather was favorable, and the next day there would be a very strong headwind which would significantly slow me down.

But in the afternoon another accident happened, and this time I was not even sure if I would be able to continue the trip. This time it was my bike – the cable for my rear shifter broke and the bike stuck on the hardest gear. At first, I was disappointed and a bit angry that I did not check it before starting the whole trip. But I quickly found a decent solution – I just made a knot on the cable, so the transmission would stay on the middle gear. Not perfect, but at least I could keep going and shift my front gear. On a flat surface and small uphills, it was nice, but on steeper ones, I had to walk, and there were a lot of them.

At some point, Matteo caught up with me and told me that he found an available cabin, and I agreed to share it. It was located just halfway of that west-oriented section, but I decided that it would be nice to sleep inside. He went faster since the check-in time was limited, and I went even slower than usual because of my gear. According to the map, the last kilometers before the cabin had to be flat, but this was not correct – there were at least 10 medium-sized uphills. Sometimes I could ride, but sometimes I just had to walk. Still, it did not take too much time for me – around 22 o’clock I already arrived there, but since it was very light, it did not look like late evening.

It was a lovely place near the lake and surrounded by small trees, and it even had a sauna. After we rested for a bit, we with Matteo went there. It was so nice to jump into a cold lake after it.

Later I checked my phone and saw a message from Paul – the French guy I met before. He asked if I found a good spot for the night. I invited him, but it was too late – he already found some roof and was about to sleep. And we went to sleep too.

The next day, as expected, there was a very strong western wind, and at least, no rain. Since there wasn’t a limit on daylight anymore, I did not hurry up in the morning. Slowly ate breakfast, packed my things, and started to ride. As usual, I left a bit earlier than Matteo, since he would quickly overtake me anyway. For me, it was quite hard to ride with this wind and gear, and I moved the knot on the shifter cable to change the gear to a slightly slower one.

In about an hour, I arrived at the border with Norway – the last country of our journey. I stopped to take some pictures with the sign and at that moment Matteo arrived too. We took pictures of each other and continued our journey – it was just around 15 kilometers to the next town. Also in our event guide, I found that there is a sports store there, and luckily it was open. I hoped I could buy a new cable and replace it.

So when we entered the town, I headed directly to that store. And luckily, they had exactly what I needed, and even borrowed tools so I could replace it. It was the happiest moment of the last few days – now I could use my bike normally! After I fixed my bike I went to the supermarket nearby, bought my food, and before leaving saw Paul and some other participants who just arrived. We quickly talked, they went to the supermarket and I continued riding.

Once I left the town I was even more excited about working shifter since there were a lot of uphills, and now I could enjoy them. It was the main road in the region, but luckily there were not many cars there, so I could just ride and enjoy picturesque views. And it was really beautiful. The road was surrounded by mountains and fjords, and at some point, I saw the full rainbow above the water. It was one of the most beautiful parts of the journey. The landscape was becoming more and more arctic, more empty. There were fewer and fewer trees, buildings, and people.

Luckily, earlier that day Matteo again found and reserved a cabin for us. It was in the perfect distance, so that day we had to ride exactly 180 km and leave only 100 km for the last day since we knew it would be very challenging in the end. The only issue was that check-in was only until 19:00, and we had to hurry up. For me, it was a bit hard to make it in time, so as usual, Matto went ahead and I continued riding at my own pace.

The last 5 kilometers before Olderfjord were the most challenging that day. It was a short west-oriented section, and it was still very windy and started to rain. But I knew that was the last challenge for the day and that after it there was a warm dry cabin waiting for me.

Shortly after I arrived, Paul also caught up with us, and we invited him to stay in our cabin. We even had a third bed for him. We had a small conversation but I fell asleep quickly while they were still talking. Tomorrow we finally had to finish it.

The last road north

Days 21–22 · North Cape, Norway

On the last day, we left just 100 km. We wanted to make sure we had enough time to arrive while the North Cape visitor center was still open, and we knew that we would move quite slowly because of the terrain and weather: the same as the last few days, there was a very strong west wind, and we had some west-oriented sections.

As usual, I left first. The scenery was beautiful, even though it was cloudy. The first 50 kilometers were north-oriented and covered by mountains from the west side, so I did not feel that much wind and could ride at my regular speed. In about an hour, I reached the first long tunnel – Skarvberg, almost 3 kilometers long. It was very wet and quite narrow – luckily only a few cars were passing me. After I exited the tunnel, there was a curly section of the road with a few west-oriented parts. Even though there still were some hills on the left, I already felt that strong wind and rode very slowly. I knew that there would be a few even longer west-oriented sections, but at that moment I could not anticipate what awaited me ahead. I just was riding at a comfortable speed, enjoying the views and dry weather and taking pictures.

In less than an hour I finally arrived at the most windy section of today’s route. It was 8 kilometers long, with a very strong west wind and no mountains to protect from it. First half I had to ride north with the wind coming from the left, then left, ride 2 kilometers with a headwind, and in the end, turn right and again for some time ride with the wind from the left.

At first, I just slowed down and tried to ride it at a minimal speed, as I did with previous windy sections, but very quickly I realized that it would not be possible. Even worse, before than left turn I had to climb a small hill, where the wind was even stronger. It was so strong, that it took a lot of effort to just stand, not even walk. It took me almost an hour to climb. Luckily on the top, there was some road sign, and I just leaned on it to rest for a few minutes.

Shortly I started moving again – this time almost directly against the wind. Since now I had to go downhill I thought maybe I could ride but it required even more effort than just walking. I saw 2 other participants a few hundred meters ahead – they got to the lowlands, put the bikes on the ground, and tried to rest. In half an hour I reached and passed them. They were an Asian couple, and I can only imagine how unusual was the weather and how cold it was for them. But even though it was very hard, I enjoyed that time a lot and was laughing most of the time. Right after this tough section, I had to go through the 7 kilometers of North Cape tunnel where I could finally rest after the fight with the wind.

The last few hundred meters before the tunnel were also very challenging. Very often I was pressed to the beam road barrier by my bike and could not even move for a few seconds, and later the wind took my bottle of water and it flew away to the sea. In total, it took me around 2 hours to pass this part of the route.

Near the entrance to the tunnel, I noticed a cyclist who was calling me. I came closer and saw Paul. I was surprised since I did not see him passing me. He told me, that he fell on one of the windy sections. Someone driving the camper van noticed him and offered to give him a lift. Paul agreed, and when noticed me from the car window, he asked the driver to drop him off before the tunnel and then waited for me there. It was a funny situation. Meanwhile, Matteo was still far behind.

We rested a bit and then entered the tunnel. I finally could ride my bike for some time. It was so nice and calm without the wind. Since it was the tunnel under the water, it was very deep. First half we were descending – we did it slowly on the breaks since it was very wet and slippery there. And the second half was very steep uphill. Initially, I rode it, but quickly gave up and just walked – Paul continued riding to the end of the tunnel. We met on the top, rested for a few minutes, then I decided to continue and Paul wanted to stay for longer since he was really tired and did not feel safe going outside. And indeed, it was quite dangerous to cross the bridge ahead with strong wind.

I walked on the left side to have the smaller chance be blown away into the water, and a few hundred meters later I could even ride my bike again. Since that moment the road passed mostly along the mountains on the left and I could slowly ride most of the time – the hardest section was behind I thought. Again I could enjoy the views, and once I even saw a big group of reindeer crossing the road. The good thing was that it was still very dry – but it was about to change soon.

In about an hour, I reached the last tunnel – Honningsvag. It was under the rock and was mostly flat and as usual, very wet inside. And once I exited, it started to rain. Together with the wind, it was the worst weather for cycling and at that moment I started to doubt if I should go to the finish that day – there were 30 kilometers more to go. On that island, there was a town where I could stay in a hotel for the night and continue the next day, but I was not sure if it was necessary. But by the moment I reached the intersection where I had to make that decision, I was completely wet, so I headed directly to the hotel.

In the hotel, there were a lot of tourists waiting for check-in, but finally, they gave me a room, and showed me where I could put my bike – there were many of them already, and I went to my room. It was so nice to take off wet clothes and warm up a bit. I did not regret at all the decision to stay there.

In an hour or so, Paul asked me if I booked something and I invited him – there was a double bed. He bought some food and in one more hour he arrived.

Meanwhile, Matteo just left the Northcape tunnel and could not ride any longer, so he decided to stay for the night in the toilet there.

We had plenty of sleep, and when we with Paul went to the breakfast, Matteo was already there. For me it was a bit funny to see that each of us ate a completely different meals: I took a lot of salmon, potato and some other vegetables, Paul had only a sandwich, and Matteo something else.

Around 8 we were ready to go, when all other participants had left already. I started together with Matteo and Davide, and Paul needed more time. We had just to cross the island – about 33 kilometers to go, and we finally were sure we will do it today. The weather was perfect when we started – almost no wind and sunny. So great that we did not go to the finish yesterday. On our way we saw other participants, who left early in the morning, returning already.

The distance was small, but there were around 800 meters of elevation in total, so at some point guys went ahead and I moved slower. In the middle of the island on the hills there was a small rain, but it was nothing comparing to previous day. And in the end there was the biggest hill, where I gave up and started walking again. But before the finish somehow I was able to catch up with Matteo and Davide, and we proceed to the entrance together.

And, around 11 o’clock we finally got to the globe. We were saw excited about it, started hugging and congratulated each other. Shortly after us Paul also arrived and we made some nice pictures together. We spent some time talking there and then went to the visitor center – we still had to get our stamps and the certificate. It felt very nice to finally put our bikes near the wall and enter the building – finally the journey was complete, and we did not have to hurry.

The way home

We got our stamps, bought some souvenirs in the shop and went to the restaurant. We spent a few hours sitting and chatting there and decided how we were going to return home now and where to sleep that night. Again, Matteo did a good job there. He found a ship to Tromso, where then we could stay for some time and take flights back home.

Also, he found out that a friend of his relative works in the visitor center, and they allowed us to stay in their apartment in Honninsvag for the night. They did not have a nice room for the guests, but we happily agreed to sleep in the laundry room on the floor – it was much better than outside.

Then we tried to negotiate with bus drivers about giving us a lift back to the town, but none of them had space for us with bikes, so in the end we had to ride 33 kilometers back. It was not that hard, but again it started to rain and we got a bit wet. Around 8 we arrived at the house, prepared our place for sleep, went outside for dinner, and fell asleep.

The next day we needed to wake up early since the ship was leaving around 5:30. When we came to the port, we were surprised how small it was, and when our huge ship arrived, it looked even more strange.

The way to Tromso would take around 15 hours, and initially, we considered reserving a room, but it was quite expensive, and the ticket itself wasn’t cheap. So we just stayed in the common area at the front of the ship. The views were very beautiful, but when we entered the open sea, I started to feel bad. Luckily, on the back of the ship, there was a big terrace, and I tried to spend most of the time there in the fresh air together with Paul.

We made a lot of stops in very beautiful places, and every time I was surprised at how small were the ports compared to the ship. Around 20 o’clock we arrived at Tromso, when it was getting darker already. Together with other participants who took the same ship, we headed to the same hotel nearby, quickly checked in, and finally lay down. Matteo had a flight the next morning, and we with Paul decided to stay for one more night to see the town and rest.

In the morning Matteo came to us to say goodbye. We hugged, said a few words to each other and he left. And I again fell asleep. We also asked him where he plans to get a box for the bike, and he told us that there is a huge commercial center with sports shops near the airport, so maybe we can ask for a box there and then walk to the airport.

I woke up in the afternoon already and went for a walk around the city. I’ve been there a few years ago already in winter, and now there was perfect sunny weather. The center is quite small there, but I really enjoyed walking at the quay near the modern buildings. I even thought that it would be great to live there.

In the evening we met with Paul and wanted to eat dinner together, but we could not find anything nice and not expensive at the same time. We found a beer place, where they did not have any food, but the barman told us that we could bring the food from other places there as long as we ordered a beer. So we ordered shawarmas across the road, returned there, and had a nice relaxed evening.

The next morning we had our flight to Oslo. We left the hotel a bit earlier, rode to that shopping center Matteo told us about, found the boxes in one of the sports shops, and walked for 15 minutes to the airport. In Oslo, Paul planned to take a bus with transfers to Paris, and I wanted to stay for a few nights to see the city, visit my friend Max, and then take a flight to Madrid.

We checked in our bikes without any issues. The flight took a few hours, and in Oslo, we took the quite expensive, but quick train from the airport to the train station. And there our journey together has come to an end. We stayed for some time there while waiting for Max and then we said goodbye to each other: Paul headed to the bus station and we with Max to my hostel. I put my bike there and we went to a pub to drink some beer and eat something.

Next day we went to the beach together with Max and his girlfriend, spent a nice afternoon there, and in the evening I said goodbye to them too. The next morning Max helped me to bring by bike to the train station and from there I went back to the airport and took a direct flight home.

It truly was one of the best and longest adventures of my life. At the beginning I wasn’t sure if I would make it to the end in time, but I quickly adapted to that routine and did not feel difficulties anymore. But when I returned home at the same time it was very nice to not worry about where I will sleep and the minimal distance to ride for the day. I finished this article 1.5 years after finishing that journey, so I could forget some details, but I plan to make a similar trip this summer, and this time I will try to write notes daily. If someone doubts about joining something like this – I can really recommend doing it.
